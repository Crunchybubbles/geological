This increment asks whether the Phase 1 body/history result can resolve into a coherent bulk-rock parcel without storing mineralogy per block. It deliberately reuses the existing rift-to-arc geometry and deposit proof rather than adding another body or deposit family.

## Typed material catalog

`phase2-materials.json` is a public classpath data pack with a strict boundary. Duplicate JSON keys, trailing documents, missing or unknown fields, invalid enum values, unsupported elements, malformed namespaced IDs, unsafe citation URIs, non-closing constituent modes, unresolved constituent references, and incomplete lithology/overprint coverage all prevent Phase 2 world creation.

The first catalog contains:

- 50 ideal mineral/endmember definitions needed by the existing rock, alteration, sulfide, weathering, placer, igneous, sedimentary, surficial, metamorphic, and mantle-cargo proof;
- two typed non-crystalline constituents—organic matter and rhyolitic volcanic glass—with exact authored bulk element mass fractions, separate from the mineral registry;
- eight ideal solid-solution definitions for plagioclase, biotite, calcic amphibole, hornblende, calcic clinopyroxene, olivine, orthopyroxene, and garnet;
- one primary assemblage, typed texture, exact coarse sediment-yield spectrum, and bounded physical-property distribution record for every one of the 38 currently implemented `Lithology` values;
- one metamorphic, hydrothermal, or weathering response for every implemented `Overprint` value, with a typed response texture for isochemical metamorphism and non-isochemical target assemblages conditioned by exact protolith-family coverage.

Authored modes are central constituent-volume parts per million and must sum to one million. Each rock also declares one or more named modal-variation axes. An axis is a sparse set of signed constituent loadings that must sum to zero; cumulative loading on each constituent may not exceed the rock's declared modal-spread fraction. These axes encode substitution or mixture directions instead of allowing every constituent to vary independently. Rocks additionally declare a coarse primary texture class, an exact gravel-and-coarser/sand/fines sediment-yield spectrum, and triangular minimum/mode/maximum distributions for porosity, permeability, and erodibility. Schema v9 requires the sediment-yield fractions to be non-negative and close to exactly one million; no rock may inherit a hidden default. It also requires every primary metamorphic rock to author a resolvable non-metamorphic original protolith plus grade, facies, P-T path, and bounded peak P-T envelope, and rejects this state on non-metamorphic recipes. Every overprint declares a nullable response texture, which must be present exactly for isochemical metamorphism. Ideal mineral formulas use a bounded element vocabulary; non-crystalline constituents instead author exact bulk element mass fractions. Whole-rock element mass fractions are derived from constituent chemistry, modal volume, and density, then closed to exactly one million parts with deterministic largest-remainder rounding. Endmember modes participating in a declared solid solution remain the authoritative bulk-composition proxies, but queries additionally interpret their combined mode as one compositional phase. The catalog labels its modes, sediment yields, covariance loadings, protolith-conditioned response targets, P-T envelopes, spread bounds, primary and resolved textures, and property distributions as proof tunables pending geological review; it does not present them as measured universal rock compositions or universally calibrated weathering products.

## Derived query state

`Phase2World.create(seed)` returns a `MaterialQueryEngine`. The finite matrix of 38 lithologies and nine overprints is validated into immutable recipe templates once per engine. Each geological body receives one deterministic triangular score per authored modal-variation axis; applying the signed loadings produces correlated primary modes while triangular property distributions independently resolve porosity, permeability, and erodibility. All streams are keyed by world identity, stable body ID, and explicit property purpose, so querying another point or clearing a cache cannot consume or perturb a result. Integer modes close exactly to one million parts using largest-remainder allocation with domain-separated deterministic tie-breaking. A bounded cache retains resolved body/overprint recipes without becoming part of the scientific state.

A point query first obtains the immutable Phase 1 geological sample and then derives a `PetrologicSample` containing:

- the primary and resolved material-constituent assemblages;
- primary and resolved solid-solution phase states;
- constituent-derived primary and resolved bulk element composition;
- an exact local element-delta ledger and causal process attribution;
- optional typed process-fluid conditions for hydrothermal and weathering responses;
- primary and resolved rock texture;
- porosity, permeability, and erodibility response;
- original-protolith-aware metamorphic grade, facies, and compact P-T-t path;
- an optional linked magma-lineage pulse state;
- optional mantle-cargo source, fertility, diamond-grade, and indicator-mineral state for kimberlitic carriers;
- an optional sedimentary facies, maturity, diagenesis, and named-provenance state.

The three existing pluton pulses share the province magma-system ID and progress from diorite through granodiorite to the felsic stock. Rock names are therefore outputs of one ordered differentiation abstraction, not independent random rolls. Existing basin members retain basement provenance; the marine volcaniclastic member additionally names the magmatic lineage.

Each `SolidSolutionState` reports its total phase mode, exact one-million-part endmember volume fractions, formula-mass-corrected mole fractions, interpolated ideal formula, phase bulk composition, density, hardness, and weathering resistance. Component normalization uses deterministic largest-remainder allocation. The joins are albite–anorthite plagioclase, phlogopite–annite biotite, tremolite–ferro-actinolite calcic amphibole, simplified hydroxyl magnesiohornblende–ferrohornblende, diopside–hedenbergite calcic clinopyroxene, forsterite–fayalite olivine, enstatite–ferrosilite orthopyroxene, and pyrope–almandine garnet. These endmember relations are supported by published work on [plagioclase](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/3581129/), [phlogopite–annite](https://rruff.info/doclib/am/vol77/AM77_1191.pdf), [tremolite–ferro-actinolite](https://rruff.info/doclib/am/vol83/AM83_458.pdf), the [USGS hornblende formula and solid-solution summary](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2003/ofr-03-395/DESCRIPT/M/hornblende_hs115.html), [calcic clinopyroxene](https://rruff.info/uploads/MM37_216.pdf), [olivine](https://rruff.info/uploads/AM53_807.pdf), [pyroxenes](https://rruff.info/uploads/AM66_1.pdf), and USGS documentation of kimberlitic [pyrope–almandine xenocrysts](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr83156). The model is deliberately ideal: it does not yet calculate activities, site ordering, miscibility gaps, solvi, or exsolution. This preserves an explicit upgrade path rather than presenting linear interpolation as equilibrium thermodynamics.

The first content-breadth slice adds komatiitic/ultramafic, basaltic, and gabbroic behavior classes. It distinguishes ultramafic crystalline, volcanic aphanitic, and intrusive phaneritic textures while resolving plagioclase, olivine, orthopyroxene, clinopyroxene, and magnetite proportions. The relationships follow [USGS stratiform-chromite host petrology](https://doi.org/10.3133/sir20105070E), a [USGS olivine-gabbro study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/petrology-and-chemistry-green-acres-gabbro-complex-near-winchester-riverside-county), published [gabbro modal ranges](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70016308), and [USGS basalt petrography](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0214d/report.pdf). The exact modes, covariance axes, and physical-property distributions remain project proof tunables pending catalog review. These definitions are queryable and participate in every material-response validation, but Phase 1's current rift-to-arc geometry does not yet emit them as additional bodies.

The second breadth slice adds siltstone, limestone, dolostone, and chert. Siltstone provides a clastic-silt state between the existing sandstone and shale recipes; limestone and dolostone separate calcite- and dolomite-dominant carbonate chemistry; chert adds a low-porosity microcrystalline-silica behavior. Each class has explicit facies, grain-size, maturity, and diagenesis state. The relationships follow USGS descriptions of [siltstone framework and matrix](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/1007/report.pdf), [limestone and dolostone mineralogy](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/2195/b2195.pdf), [dolomitization](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/dolomitization-quaternary-reef-limestones-aitutaki-cook-islands), and [microcrystalline quartz in chert](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2004/1098/). Exact modes, covariance, and physical properties remain review tunables. Phase 1 geometry does not yet emit these additional facies, and its current source-body field records only the modeled terrigenous contribution rather than a typed seawater/biogenic reservoir.

The third breadth slice adds gypsum/anhydrite and halite/potash evaporite behavior classes, the gypsum, anhydrite, halite, and sylvite mineral definitions, and chlorine to the formula vocabulary. The sulfate class represents earlier gypsum/anhydrite precipitation and hydration–recrystallization state; the chloride class is halite-rich with a later sylvite component and records salt recrystallization, dissolution, and halokinesis as post-depositional behavior. The relationships follow the USGS [chemical-sedimentary deposit models](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2007/1214/PDF/8.0-chemical-sedimentry-FINAL.pdf), [global potash overview](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2010/5090/s/), [Prairie Evaporite mineralogy](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/sir20105090CC), and [evaporite-dissolution hazard synthesis](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70029326). Exact modes, covariance, mineral physical properties, and rock physical-property distributions remain review tunables. These definitions are queryable and participate in all material-response validation, but they do not claim that present-day aridity alone creates an evaporite basin: Phase 1 geometry does not yet implement restricted-basin concentration/reflooding gates or emit these facies. The existing sedimentary source-body field also remains a terrigenous-provenance placeholder rather than a typed brine reservoir.

The fourth breadth slice adds a subtype-neutral banded iron-formation behavior and the siderite ideal endmember. Its banded iron–silica texture and central recipe resolve fine-grained quartz, magnetite, hematite, siderite, and minor carbonate, sulfide, and clay components. USGS descriptions give this shared core mineralogy while separating laterally extensive Superior-type sheets in stable shallow-marine shelf/intracratonic settings from smaller Algoma-type beds in volcano-sedimentary greenstone basins ([Superior model 34a](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull971h.htm); [Algoma model 28b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull1h45.htm)). The texture records centimetre-scale banding as material metadata rather than claiming literal block-scale laminae. Exact modes, covariance, siderite physical properties, and rock properties remain proof tunables. Phase 1 geometry does not emit this lithology, and neither an ancient age nor the catalog recipe selects a BIF subtype or proves formation: later body generation still requires the appropriate ocean-redox, iron-supply, low-clastic-dilution, basin, and preservation gates. Supergene silica leaching and residual iron enrichment also remain a separate future process rather than an ore grade implied by the primary recipe.

The fifth breadth slice adds coal without representing its organic fraction as a fictitious mineral. Authoring schema v6 introduces a general material-constituent boundary and a separate non-crystalline registry. The initial organic-matter definition has exact authored C–H–N–O–S bulk chemistry and density; it is a deterministic proof proxy, not a universal coal analysis. The coal recipe combines that constituent with kaolinite, quartz, muscovite, chlorite, pyrite, calcite, and siderite as mineral matter and partings. This distinction follows the USGS description of coal as organic matter plus inorganic mineral components and its separation of organically bound elements from discrete minerals ([Coal: A Complex Natural Resource](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/c1143/html/text.html); [geologic controls on mineral matter](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/geologic-factors-control-mineral-matter-coal)). The bulk-element fields align with the C, H, O, N, and S categories used in ultimate analysis ([USGS Circular 735 methods](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/c735/method.htm)). The sedimentary state records a buried-peat mire origin, organic bedding with clastic partings, compaction/dewatering, and burial maturation while deliberately leaving rank unresolved. Phase 1 does not yet generate peat bodies or supply the climate, hydrology, vegetation, accommodation, preservation, burial, or thermal-history gates required to form and rank coal, so catalog availability alone cannot create a coal seam.

The sixth breadth slice adds a shale-derived pelitic progression from the combined slate/phyllite behavior class to mica schist. Slate/phyllite is low-grade greenschist-facies material with a slaty-to-phyllitic texture and a quartz–white-mica–chlorite recipe containing minor graphite, carbonate, sulfide, and oxide. Mica schist is a medium-grade foliated product with quartz, muscovite, ideal biotite endmembers, plagioclase, remnant chlorite, and an almandine index-mineral proxy. USGS defines phyllite as intermediate in grade between slate and mica schist and identifies fine sericite/chlorite/graphite as characteristic sheen-forming minerals ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=5.4&thcode=4)); mapped phyllites are commonly quartz–sericite–chlorite rocks with minor graphite ([USGS Bulletin 1161-F](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1161f/report.pdf)). Published USGS pelitic sequences document quartz + muscovite + plagioclase + chlorite + biotite with the first appearance of almandine at the garnet isograd ([USGS Open-File Report 81-593](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1981/0593/report.pdf)), and the almandine ideal formula is `Fe3Al2Si3O12` ([USGS Spectral Library](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2003/ofr-03-395/DESCRIPT/M/almandine_hs114.html)). Exact modes, mineral properties, and P-T envelopes remain proof tunables rather than universal boundaries. Phase 1 currently emits only the basement gneiss as a primary metamorphic body and does not yet resolve regional grade/strain fields, reaction progress, devolatilization, retrogression, or slate-to-schist spatial transitions.

The seventh breadth slice adds basalt-derived greenschist and amphibolite behavior classes. Greenschist is a low-grade, schistose chlorite–epidote–albite–actinolite assemblage with minor quartz, carbonate, and magnetite. Amphibolite is a medium-grade, nematoblastic hornblende–plagioclase assemblage with subordinate quartz, chlorite, epidote, biotite, almandine, and magnetite. USGS describes actinolite and epidote as abundant in greenschist-facies metabasalt ([USGS Bulletin 2123](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b2123/meta.html)) and reports amphibolite assemblages dominated by hornblende and plagioclase with quartz, biotite, epidote, chlorite, or garnet as accessory phases ([USGS Open-File Report 03-221](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2003/of03-221/data/welldata/xwellsite-faq.htm)). The catalog therefore keeps the existing lower-aluminum tremolite–ferro-actinolite phase for greenschist and adds separate simplified hydroxyl magnesiohornblende–ferrohornblende endmembers for amphibolite. Exact modes, ideal endmember properties, covariance, and P-T envelopes remain proof tunables; the hornblende proxy omits sodium, fluorine, site ordering, and other real amphibole complexity. Phase 1 does not yet emit either class or resolve a basalt-to-greenschist-to-amphibolite reaction-progress field.

The eighth breadth slice extends that basalt-derived progression to high-grade mafic granulite. Its granoblastic, relatively anhydrous proxy contains calcic plagioclase, orthopyroxene, clinopyroxene, almandine, quartz, and magnetite rather than retaining amphibolite's dominant hornblende. USGS descriptions of mafic granulite report medium- to coarse-grained granoblastic orthopyroxene–clinopyroxene–plagioclase assemblages with quartz or olivine only locally ([USGS Open-File Report 2007-1047](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2007/1047/ea/of2007-1047ea046.pdf)); another USGS study reports garnet–clinopyroxene–plagioclase mafic granulites with minor orthopyroxene, quartz, feldspar, amphibole, biotite, oxides, and accessory phases ([USGS Bulletin 1604](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1604/report.pdf)). The catalog combines their shared two-pyroxene, plagioclase, and garnet signal as a broad behavior class rather than asserting one universal natural assemblage. Its exact modal recipe, covariance, physical properties, and `700–900 °C`/`500–1200 MPa` proof envelope remain tunable. Phase 1 does not yet emit this class or calculate dehydration, partial melting, pressure-dependent garnet production, retrogression, or a spatial amphibolite-to-granulite transition.

The ninth breadth slice adds granoblastic quartzite and marble as contrasting metasedimentary products. Quartzite retains the basin-sandstone protolith and has an 84-percent central quartz mode with subordinate feldspar, mica, carbonate, and oxides. USGS defines quartzite as a mainly quartz granoblastic rock recrystallized from sandstone or chert ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=5.2.3&thcode=4)), and mapped metasedimentary nomenclature uses at least 80 percent quartz for quartzite ([USGS I-1898-E](https://pubs.usgs.gov/imap/1898e/report.pdf)). Marble retains the limestone protolith and has a 92-percent central calcite-plus-dolomite mode with minor quartz, mica, graphite, sulfide, and oxide. USGS defines scientific marble as recrystallized limestone composed predominantly of interlocking calcite and/or dolomite grains ([USGS Bulletin 1109](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/1109/report.pdf)) and describes its common granoblastic or saccharoidal texture ([USGS Open-File Report 2005-1235](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2005/1235/of2005-1235.pdf)). Both definitions use one low-grade greenschist-facies regional P-T path for the proof; neither rock name universally implies that grade or facies, and both can form under regional or thermal metamorphism. Exact modes, covariance, and physical properties remain tunable. Phase 1 does not yet transform sandstone or limestone bodies through a spatial reaction-progress field or distinguish pure marble from calc-silicate reaction products.

The tenth breadth slice makes hornfels an explicit host-preserving behavior rather than adding a duplicate fixed-composition primary `Lithology`. The USGS defines hornfels as a fine-grained mosaic of equidimensional grains without preferred orientation, typically produced by contact metamorphism ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=5.1&thcode=4)), while mapped hornfels mineralogy depends on the original rock ([USGS Open-File Report 2006-1259](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2006/1259/metamor_geo.html)). The `CONTACT_HORNFELS` overprint therefore resolves any host's texture to `HORNFELSIC`, raises its metamorphic state to the existing hornblende-hornfels facies and `500–700 °C`/`100–300 MPa` proof envelope, and modifies porosity and erodibility while retaining primary constituent modes and exact whole-rock element inventory. The host-preserving composition is consistent with a USGS contact study in which uniform fine-grained shale became andalusite hornfels with little bulk-composition change ([USGS contact-metamorphism study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/contact-metamorphism-adjacent-a-teschenite-intrusion)). The fabric and P-T values remain coarse proof tunables: this slice does not calculate protolith-specific contact reactions, mineral growth, devolatilization, partial melting, aureole zoning, or reaction progress.

The eleventh breadth slice adds serpentinite as the hydrated metamorphic product of the komatiitic/ultramafic class. USGS defines serpentinite as a rock composed almost wholly of serpentine-group minerals formed by hydrating ferromagnesian silicates such as olivine and pyroxene ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=5.14&thcode=4)). Its central proof recipe is 85 percent lizardite, chrysotile, and antigorite, with brucite and magnetite plus minor relict forsterite, enstatite, and chlorite; a mesh/relict texture records replacement of the original grains. This follows the USGS synthesis of direct ultramafic hydration and the common lizardite–chrysotile–brucite–magnetite assemblage ([Coleman 1971](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/petrologic-and-geophysical-nature-serpentinites)) and documented mesh textures after olivine and pyroxene ([USGS Professional Paper 600-B](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/0600b/report.pdf)). The initial `SUBGREENSCHIST`/`HYDROTHERMAL_HYDRATION` path uses a deliberately shallow `25–300 °C` and `1–10 MPa` proof envelope matching a published USGS water–rock simulation domain ([USGS ultramafic-system models](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/geochemical-models-metasomatism-ultramafic-systems-serpentinization-rodingitization)). Lizardite, chrysotile, and antigorite retain separate phase IDs but currently share the simplified ideal `Mg3Si2O5(OH)4` composition. The class therefore exposes structurally bound hydrogen and reduced bulk density while preserving ultramafic provenance, but it is a final-rock snapshot rather than a mass-balanced serpentinization contribution: reaction progress, water provenance and consumption, volume expansion, reducing fluid/H2 production, silica or magnesium transfer, antigorite stability at higher P-T, asbestos-fiber habit, and later carbonation remain outside this increment.

The twelfth breadth slice adds crystalline andesitic and rhyolitic volcanic behavior between basalt and the existing intrusive lineage. Andesite is plagioclase-dominant and porphyritic, with hornblende, clinopyroxene, orthopyroxene, minor olivine and biotite, and iron oxide in a fine-grained matrix. This broad proxy follows USGS Pinatubo dome andesites containing 39–42 volume-percent phenocrysts dominated by plagioclase, hornblende, clinopyroxene, olivine, and Fe-Ti oxides ([Pinatubo petrology](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/bernard/)) and mapped andesite containing plagioclase, pyroxene, hornblende, biotite, olivine, and opaque oxides ([USGS Bulletin 2064-II](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/2064/ii/508b2064ii.html)). Rhyolite uses a felsitic flow-banded texture and a quartz–alkali-feldspar–sodic-plagioclase recipe with minor biotite and magnetite. Its central QAP fraction contains 40 percent quartz and a plagioclase fraction of about 32 percent of total feldspar, within the USGS-cited modal rhyolite field; the same source defines rhyolite by quartz and alkali-feldspar phenocrysts with lesser plagioclase and biotite in microcrystalline or glassy groundmass ([USGS Open-File Report 2005-1235](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2005/1235/of2005-1235.pdf)). The flow-banded stony proxy follows documented dense rhyolite cores in which deformation during emplacement aligns crystals and glass ([Yellowstone Volcano Observatory](https://www.usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/rock-glass-and-flowbands-yellowstones-rhyolite-anatomy)). Exact modes and physical properties remain proof tunables. These two classes are catalog-queryable but are not yet emitted by Phase 1 bodies or joined into the existing pluton-only `MagmaLineageState`; glassy carapaces, obsidian, vesicles, spherulites, dacite, and compositional zoning remain separate future behavior.

The thirteenth breadth slice adds separate alkaline and carbonatitic magmatic host-rock behavior. The alkaline class is deliberately one quartz-free nepheline-syenite proxy: 75 central volume-percent alkali feldspar plus nepheline, with albite, aegirine, phlogopite, magnetite, and minor fluorapatite. USGS describes nepheline syenite as essentially quartz-free and composed of alkali feldspar and nepheline ([USGS Minerals Yearbook](https://pubs.usgs.gov/myb/vol1/2019/myb1-2019-feldspar.pdf)); mapped alkaline complexes additionally show wide microperthite–nepheline proportions and sodic pyroxene/amphibole accessories ([USGS Professional Paper 1269](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/1269/report.pdf)). The carbonatite proxy contains 83 central volume-percent calcite plus dolomite, exceeding the greater-than-50-percent primary-carbonate definition and retaining the dominant carbonate pair described by USGS ([carbonatite REE geology](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/rare-earth-element-ore-geology-carbonatites)). Fluorapatite introduces explicit phosphorus and fluorine chemistry alongside magnetite, phlogopite, aegirine, and pyrite accessories documented in carbonatite complexes ([USGS Professional Paper 1049-F](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/1049f/report.pdf)). Both classes have distinct primary textures, mass-conserving modal-variation axes, and proof-level physical-property distributions. They are catalog-queryable but not yet emitted by a rare-igneous body generator or connected to `MagmaLineageState`; ring complexes, dikes, breccias, sodic amphiboles, fenitization, liquid immiscibility, REE/Nb ore minerals and budgets, and deposit fertility remain future causal state rather than implied by the rock name.

The fourteenth breadth slice adds a kimberlitic carrier-rock proxy while refusing to manufacture diamond fertility from its name. Its macrocrystic volatile-rich recipe contains 34 central volume-percent olivine, a serpentine–phlogopite–calcite groundmass, diopside, ilmenite, perovskite, magnetite, fluorapatite, and minor sulfide. That broad assemblage follows the USGS definition of kimberlite as porphyritic alkalic peridotite with abundant olivine and phlogopite in fine calcite-bearing groundmass ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=4.7.1.2&thcode=4)) and a documented kimberlite containing 25–30 modal-percent olivine in phlogopite–diopside–serpentine–perovskite–spinel matrix ([Homestead kimberlite](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70026760)). The catalog adds diamond, pyrope, chromite, ilmenite, and perovskite minerals plus chromium chemistry and an ideal pyrope–almandine garnet join. Diamond, pyrope, and chromite are absent from the primary carrier modes. Instead, every kimberlitic query carries a separate `MantleCargoState` naming diamond and the pyrope/chromite/ilmenite/diopside indicator candidates. The current status is `SOURCE_CONTEXT_UNRESOLVED`, with no source reservoir and exactly zero reported diamond grade, because Phase 1 has no thick-cratonic-keel reservoir or older diamond-forming event. This is unknown rather than barren. Future `BARREN` or `DIAMOND_BEARING` status requires a resolved source reservoir; diamond grade is stored as ppb by mass so the approximately `0.01–0.20 ppm` economic range remains representable ([USGS diamond-bearing pipe model](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1991/ofr-91-0011-a/ofr-91-0011a.pdf)). USGS reports that only about 10 percent of known kimberlites are diamond-bearing and only 1–2 percent are economic, and it requires compositionally screened indicators rather than rock-name inference ([USGS SIR 2016-5089-A](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2016/5089/a/sir20165089a.pdf)). Pipe/diatreme geometry, crater/root facies, xenolith populations, keel intersection, ascent preservation, indicator-mineral compositional screening, diamond size/quality distributions, and placer release remain future causal state.

The fifteenth breadth slice completes the planned primary igneous behavior list with a welded, glass-rich pyroclastic class. Its `WELDED_FRAGMENTAL` texture and central recipe contain 60 volume-percent separately typed rhyolitic volcanic glass, with quartz, alkali feldspar, plagioclase, biotite, magnetite, and hornblende crystal fragments. USGS describes volcanic ash as varying mixtures of volcanic glass, crystals, and rock fragments and identifies glass shards as quenched melt rather than minerals ([USGS ash components](https://volcanoes.usgs.gov/volcanic_ash/components_ash.html)). Welded tuff is specifically a glass-rich pyroclastic rock indurated as its hot glass shards weld together ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=3.2.1.1&thcode=4)); ash-flow deposits are commonly dominated by glass shards and pumice, with welding reducing original porosity ([USGS Basin and Range aquifers](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ha/ha730/ch_b/B-text2.html)). The catalog therefore types glass separately from crystalline minerals and treats this rock as primary `IGNEOUS` material, while the older marine volcaniclastic class remains `SEDIMENTARY`, lacks glass in its central proxy, and retains named source-to-sink and burial state. The dry glass chemistry, modes, covariance, and physical properties are proof tunables rather than a universal tuff analysis. Phase 1 does not yet emit eruption deposits or resolve a source vent, fall versus flow transport, pumice/lithic fractions, cooling-unit zoning, degree of welding, devitrification, vapor-phase crystallization, hydrothermal alteration, or later sedimentary reworking, so catalog-only pyroclastic queries deliberately have neither magma-lineage nor sedimentary provenance state.

The sixteenth breadth slice completes the initial approximately 38-class catalog with laterite/bauxite, soil/colluvium, and glacial-till behavior alongside the existing alluvial gravel and host-preserving saprolitic weathering response. The lateritic class has a residual pisolitic texture and a central 42 volume-percent gibbsite-plus-boehmite mode with kaolinite, goethite, hematite, quartz, and minor relict phases. USGS defines laterite-type bauxite as weathered residual subsoil dominated by gibbsite or gibbsite–boehmite mixtures with hematite, goethite, anatase, and local quartz, commonly in pisolitic, massive, nodular, or earthy form ([USGS model 38b](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/b1693/html/bull21ut.htm)); its tabulated formulas and physical ranges support separate `Al(OH)3` gibbsite and `AlO(OH)` boehmite definitions ([USGS Professional Paper 1076-B](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/1076b/report.pdf)). The soil/colluvium proxy is a loose heterogeneous weathered mineral mixture, consistent with the USGS definition of colluvium as soil and rock fragments moved by rainwash, sheetwash, or downslope creep ([USGS lithology thesaurus](https://apps.usgs.gov/thesaurus/term-simple.php?code=1.5.1&thcode=4)); mapped USGS colluvium likewise consists of poorly sorted slope material transported by gravity and sheet erosion whose clast composition reflects its bedrock or surficial source ([USGS Open-File Report 93-699](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1993/0699/report.pdf)). USGS mapping also distinguishes source-controlled colluvium derived from sandstone/quartzite from matrix contributed by weathered or transported fines ([USGS I-2789](https://pubs.usgs.gov/imap/i-2789/i-2789_p.pdf)), while Piedmont diamicton is reported to derive from underlying saprolite, weathered rock, and bedrock ([USGS regolith study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/regoliths-middle-atlantic-piedmont-and-evolution-a-polymorphic-landscape)). Till is a distinct unsorted, unstratified clay-to-boulder glacial deposit with a matrix-limited permeability range well below sorted alluvial gravel ([USGS glacial aquifer summary](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ha/ha730/ch_j/J-text1.html)). These catalog recipes contain no native gold and do not assert bauxite or placer ore. Their modes and physical properties remain proof tunables. Phase 2 can now form a coarse source-linked colluvial mantle from existing slope, weathering, drainage, local-bedrock, and bounded upslope-bedrock evidence, but Phase 1 still lacks soil horizons and organics or a routed, conservative sediment-transport budget; it also lacks the tropical-weathering/climate and profile-preservation gates needed for laterite and the ice history needed for till. Saprolite remains compositionally tied to its parent through `WEATHERED_REGOLITH` instead of becoming a parentless fixed recipe.

Contact hornfels is an isochemical response: it changes resolved texture, P-T-t, and physical state without changing primary modes or whole-rock element inventory. Potassic, phyllic, propylitic, chloritic, gossan, regolith, and unconformity-weathering responses select exactly one target recipe for the host's `GeneticFamily`, blend its assemblage, and expose exact signed element deltas. The compact catalog now distinguishes crystalline igneous/metamorphic, sedimentary, hydrothermal, and—where preservation semantics differ—surficial targets. The relationships follow the established quartz–K-feldspar/biotite, quartz–sericite–pyrite, chlorite–epidote–carbonate, chloritic VMS-footwall, iron-oxide gossan, and quartz/clay/oxide regolith associations summarized by [USGS porphyry models](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2008/1321/pdf/OF081321_508.pdf), the [USGS Arctic VMS study](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/mineral-and-whole-rock-compositions-seawater-dominated-hydrothermal-alteration-arctic), and a [USGS granitic-saprolite study](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2006/1366/). Exact target proportions remain tunable. Surficial gossan/regolith targets retain the placer parcel's one-part-per-million native-gold carrier through integer blending, while hydrothermal gossan targets contain no gold and cannot manufacture an unbudgeted commodity. A typed `MaterialProcessLedger` separates the positive and negative sides of that normalized delta, names the causal aureole, hydrothermal system, or weathering system, and carries matching chronicle events when the supplied geological history contains them. Its additions and removals balance because both bulk compositions are normalized; it is process attribution, not a claim that the local parcel is an absolute closed mass reservoir.

Hydrothermal and weathering definitions must also author a `ProcessFluidState`; inactive and contact-isochemical responses must omit it. The pH bins correspond to `<3`, `3–5.5`, `5.5–8.5`, `8.5–10.5`, and `>10.5`; salinity bins correspond to `<0.5`, `0.5–3.5`, `3.5–10`, `10–30`, and `>30` wt% NaCl equivalent. ## Column and surface projection

`MaterialQueryEngine.column` projects the existing Phase 1 interval proof rather than sampling every block again. It resolves one material state per geological run, retains the underlying transition/candidate/complexity evidence, and returns contiguous `PetrologicRun` values. Exhaustive block-center tests prove that every compressed state equals an independent point-material query, including across cache eviction and fresh engine construction.

`MaterialQueryEngine.surface` resolves the existing Phase 1 surface fields into one of four explicit relationships: exposed bedrock, in-situ regolith, transported colluvial mantle, or transported alluvial placer. Bedrock and regolith retain their source body identity. A non-channel, non-outcrop parcel with at least `0.10` model slope, `4.0` model weathering depth, and `32` blocks of channel separation becomes a young (`0.02 Ma`) soil/colluvium body. Its deterministic body identity differs from the underlying bedrock. Phase 2 obtains the initial terrain gradient from bounded four-block central differences of the existing surface elevation and normalizes the uphill vector; this deliberately begins on the gravity-driven hillslope fall line rather than the longitudinal drainage-trunk direction. Every subsequent `32`-block reach repeats that stencil at its current point and advances along the updated local uphill vector; an exactly flat intermediate stencil retains the preceding bearing. To keep fixed-step ridge overshoot from folding the route back on itself, every updated bearing is clipped to at most `60` degrees from the initial direction. Each reach therefore retains at least half its length as forward projection. Every `TerrainPathReach` records the raw local gradient, the routed bearing actually used, and whether flat-terrain fallback or deflection clipping was applied, so route decisions remain inspectable rather than being reconstructed from endpoints. At the matrix/local, near, and far source points, a second bounded stencil samples the four cardinal and four diagonal elevations at an eight-block radius. Cardinal central differences define a local plane, the eight deviations from that plane produce an RMS residual slope, and `clamp(rmsResidualSlope / 0.05)` exposes a dimensionless terrain-roughness index without treating ordinary planar slope as roughness. `ColluvialSourceMix` exposes the initial direction and three canonical bedrock-source tranches at `0`, `96`, and `192` blocks of cumulative routed distance. One shared adaptive polyline supplies the local, near, and far `TerrainPath` records with one, four, and seven ordered point/elevation observations without duplicate centerline queries. The path schema proves exact `32`-block segment geometry and shared prefixes, and each source contribution must terminate at its corresponding path endpoint. Each tranche also retains its owning province, body ID, lithology, overprint, and deposited assemblage fraction. The bounded route may cross a province boundary, and each source is resolved against the province owning its endpoint. Repeated body IDs remain separate distance tranches, while the public source-body list is distinct and sorted. The deposited fractions are no longer the initial `35/20/10/35` capacity split: they are derived from each input's total delivered mass as described below. `MaterialAssemblage.weightedBlend` uses those fractions in a deterministic largest-remainder reduction that closes an arbitrary number of source modes exactly and is independent of share order; bulk chemistry and solid-solution states are then recomputed. Texture is now resolved separately from the exact aggregate deposited gravel-and-coarser, sand, and fines masses rather than by reusing the bulk source fractions. `ColluvialTextureState` exposes that normalized delivered spectrum, a coarse dominance-derived sorting class, angular-to-subrounded clast shape, and matrix-, mixed-, or clast-supported fabric selected from the coarse fraction. `ColluvialHorizonState` adds the bounded weathering index and source-versus-matrix profile class without inventing absolute thickness or organic matter. The sorting class remains intentionally coarse because three bins do not resolve within-bin dispersion.

`ColluvialPhysicalState` deterministically maps that texture to porosity, permeability, and erodibility quantiles, then evaluates the existing authored soil-colluvium triangular distributions at those quantiles. Porosity starts at `0.25` and adds sorting (`0/0.20/0.35` for poor/moderate/well), support (`0.05/0/0.20` for matrix/mixed/clast), and angular-shape (`0.10`) responses. Permeability uses `0.90 gravel + 0.55 sand + 0.05 fines`, sorting adjustments of `-0.15/0/+0.15`, and support adjustments of `-0.10/0/+0.10`. Erodibility uses `0.25 gravel + 0.90 sand + 0.55 fines` with support adjustments of `0/+0.05/-0.10`. Fractions are normalized from ppm and response quantiles are clamped to the unit interval. Alpha.39 derives the coarse sorting class from the dominant deposited grain bin: below `0.55` remains unsorted-to-poorly sorted, `0.55–0.75` is moderately sorted, and at least `0.75` is well sorted; `sortingDominanceIndex` linearly maps equal three-bin proportions to `0` and a single-bin spectrum to `1`. This is a class-scale transport-selection proxy, not within-bin dispersion or a claim of hydraulic sorting. The formulas and catalog envelopes are dimensionless proof tunables, not hydraulic-conductivity measurements. They encode only defensible ordering: smaller grains that fill coarse pores and poor sorting reduce permeability, better sorting raises porosity and permeability, angularity can affect packing, and loose sand/silt-rich surface material is generally more erodible than a clast-supported framework. USGS summaries report these grain-size/sorting controls on permeability and porosity ([Nelson 1994](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/permeability-porosity-relationships-sedimentary-rocks); [Upper Klamath Basin hydrology](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2007/5050/section3.html)) and the effects of sorting and particle shape on unconsolidated-deposit porosity ([USGS Open-File Report 94-73](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1994/0073/report.pdf)). A USGS engineering-geology report also shows why the erodibility output remains a coarse index: clean and silty coarse soils are easily eroded, silty colluvial soils can be extremely susceptible, while sufficient clay can increase resistance ([USGS Open-File Report 79-1221](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1979/1221/report.pdf)). The current combined fines bin cannot resolve that silt/clay distinction.

`ColluvialSedimentBudget` partitions one parcel's `1000000 phase2_normalized_sediment_mass` capacity into explicit retained, mobilized, transport-loss, bypassed, and deposited quantities. The generic weathered matrix and local, near, and far bedrock sources begin with `350000`, `350000`, `200000`, and `100000` capacity units. For each input, mobilized capacity remains `capacity * clamp(weatheringDepth / 12) * (0.5 + 0.5 * erodibility) * (0.25 + 0.75 * clamp(sourceSlope / 0.24))`. The input's authored `SedimentGrainSize` yield partitions both capacity and mobilized mass by deterministic largest remainder. Path samples are ordered from target toward source, so a positive adjacent elevation difference is a descending reach in the source-to-target transport direction; negative differences are accumulated as intervening barrier relief. Every reach also carries its raw gradient, routed unit bearing, and fallback/clipping flags; canonical validation requires that the routed bearing reproduce the sample-to-sample geometry. For a nonlocal path, `continuity = 0.5 * (descendingReachFraction + downslopeRelief / (downslopeRelief + barrierRelief))`; a zero-relief remote path receives `0`, while a zero-length local path receives `1`. The route also exposes `routeGradeIndex = clamp((sourceElevation - targetElevation) / (routedDistance * 0.24))`, with local paths treated as fully graded for compatibility. Transport derives `pathResponse = 0.5 + 0.5 * continuity * directness * (0.75 + 0.25 * routeGradeIndex)` and `scale = (0.5 + 0.5 * clamp(sourceSlope / 0.24)) * (1 - 0.6 * terrainRoughnessIndex) * pathResponse`, a bounded `[0.1, 1.0]` response in which steeper source slopes, greater net route relief, and continuous direct downhill paths lengthen travel while rough surfaces and uphill barriers shorten it. Multiplying the gravel-and-coarser, sand, and fines reference lengths of `512`, `384`, and `256` blocks by this scale yields effective e-folding lengths of `51.2–512`, `38.4–384`, and `25.6–256` blocks; each class then survives the bounded source distance according to `exp(-distance / effectiveLength)`. The aggregate arrival deposits according to `arrival * (1 - 0.5 * clamp(targetSlope / 0.24))`, and that deposited total is apportioned across the arrived grain inventory. Retained mass is capacity minus mobilized mass, transport loss is mobilized minus arrival, and bypass is arrival minus deposition. Both `capacity = retained + mobilized` and `mobilized = transportLoss + bypass + deposited` therefore close componentwise for all three grain classes, for every input, and in aggregate. One largest-remainder reduction normalizes input deposit totals to source/matrix ppm for mineral composition; another normalizes aggregate deposited grain mass to the authoritative texture spectrum.

The coarse-longer survival ordering is a bounded dry-ravel/headwater-colluvial proof, not a universal sediment law, and the budget exposes that assumption explicitly as `SLOPE_ROUGHNESS_PATH_GRADE_RUNOFF_CONDITIONED_DRY_RAVEL_PROOF`. Its runoff input is the existing normalized flow-accumulation field, used as a deterministic runoff-availability proxy rather than a rainfall model. Each production input now also derives a bounded dominant transport-process proxy—`HILLSLOPE_CREEP`, `SHEETWASH`, or `DRY_RAVEL`—from slope, runoff availability, route grade, and route continuity; the scores and selection margin are part of the audit state, while the grain-survival law remains the explicitly named dry-ravel proof parameterization. A separate cohesion state derives fines fraction and matrix support, then publishes a coarse cohesion class and cohesion-adjusted detachment proxy instead of conflating cohesion with source erodibility. Repeated source-body claims are now aggregated into deterministic per-source usage totals, preserving exact capacity and mobilized-mass closure within the parcel while leaving cross-parcel depletion unresolved. Each source tranche now also publishes its exact deposited three-bin grain spectrum, allowing source-to-grain provenance to reconcile with the aggregate source fraction without assigning minerals to individual grain classes. The deposited three-bin spectrum now also derives bounded coarse, sand, and fines spread proxies plus a weighted dispersion class; these are an explicit resolution aid, not measured grain diameters or mineral-specific distributions. Particle experiments and modeling find that travel distance can increase with particle diameter relative to surface roughness and describe downslope coarsening on steep hillslopes; their modeled local-to-nonlocal transition also becomes sensitive to particle path history at intermediate transport scales ([DiBiase et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JF003970)). Field data likewise report downslope coarsening through talus and steep headwater-colluvial channels before fluvial fining becomes dominant ([Neely et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JF005560)). Runoff-driven hillslope work demonstrates that grain-size selection also depends nonlinearly on slope, rainfall, runoff, and initial size distribution ([Michaelides and Wainwright 2012](https://doi.org/10.1029/2012JF002415)). Terrain-flow methods motivate recomputing direction from local steepest slope instead of projecting every source along one initial bearing, although the current continuous central-difference polyline is not an implementation of D-infinity flow partitioning ([Tarboton 1997](https://doi.org/10.1029/96WR03137)). The current roughness observation is derived from mesoscale modeled elevation rather than clast-scale microtopography, and the routed continuity score is an auditable deterministic proxy rather than a calibrated connectivity index. The transported parcel does not inherit source deposit IDs, element-reservoir ledgers, or a commodity budget: any inherited source minerals are compositional fragments, not a new deposit claim. ## Deliberate limits
